# Score grey sources in eight subtotals

**Claim:** a grey source carries Garousi's eight subtotals and a total that adds up; below 10 of 20 it is an example of practice only, and says so.

- **Grade:** convention. The 20 criteria are Garousi 2019's; the threshold of 10 is an example in that paper; no study measured how reliably two persons score a source.
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 2](../../evidence/review-genre/review.md); [Garousi 2019](../../evidence/bibliography.md), Table 7 and section 5.2; [against](../../evidence/review-genre/against.md).
- **Check:** `grey-subtotals` (exact): eight subtotals within their maxima and in steps of 0.5, a total equal to their sum, and a total under 10 labelled an example. Whether a subtotal is right is judgment, and the second rater's work.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Score authority, methodology, objectivity, date, position, novelty, impact and outlet, out of 4, 6, 4, 1, 1, 2, 1, 1.
- Write the total and the eight subtotals in one row of the grey table.
- Keep a source below 10 as an example of practice, never as support for a claim.

## Why

- A grey source has no peer review; the score is its grade, as the evidence grade is a paper's.
- Grey sources change a review's result: published trials showed a 9% greater effect than grey ones (Hopewell 2007).

## Limits

- The score is one person's judgment; its reliability is unmeasured.
- The threshold is the family's reading of an example, and a standard-setting body does not score grey sources at all (Lefebvre 2025).
