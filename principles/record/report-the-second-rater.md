# Report the second rater

**Claim:** when two raters score or screen the same items, the review reports how far they agree, and how a disagreement was settled.

- **Grade:** supported with caveat for a second reader on screening in health: single reviewers missed 8% of eligible reports on average, pairs 0 to 1% (Edwards 2002). Convention for a second rating of a quality score: no study was found.
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 4](../../evidence/review-genre/review.md); [Edwards 2002](../../evidence/bibliography.md); [Waffenschmidt 2019](../../evidence/bibliography.md); [McHugh 2012](../../evidence/bibliography.md); [Page 2021](../../evidence/bibliography.md), item 8.
- **Check:** `second-rater` (exact): a section on a second rater holds an agreement figure beside the word agreement or kappa, or says the rating was not completed; whether the statistic suits the data is judgment.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Say how many raters scored or screened, and whether they worked independently (PRISMA item 8).
- Report one agreement figure, and the raw counts it rests on.
- Say how disagreements were settled, and whether the rater saw the first scores.

## Why

- Two readers catch what one misses; the loss from one is larger for a less experienced reviewer (Waffenschmidt 2019).
- Kappa can mislead on unbalanced data (McHugh 2012).

## Limits

- A model as a second rater matched humans in some tasks and was skewed by chance agreement in others (Khraisha 2024).
- A reread after the disagreement is not an independent check: the review's decisions become the reference.
