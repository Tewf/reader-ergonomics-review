# Trace every figure

**Claim:** a figure in the review names its source and where it was read, or says it is secondary, so that a reader can find it.

- **Grade:** convention. No study measured that located figures are checked more often.
- **Evidence:** [review, strand 1](../../evidence/review-genre/review.md); [Page 2021](../../evidence/bibliography.md), item 17: each included study is cited.
- **Check:** `review-numbers` (heuristic): a line with a figure holds a source key and, near it, a location or the word secondary; the role of a figure (the review's own count, a rule's constant) is judgment.
- **Reader styles:** none.

## What it asks

- Write the figure with its key and its place: abstract, section, table, page.
- Mark a figure read in an abstract or a summary as (secondary).
- Keep the review's own counts in the opening, before the first section.

## Why

- A figure with no key cannot be traced; a figure with a key and no place cannot be found in a long paper.
- In this review, 18 of 23 formal works were read from the abstract, so most of its figures are secondary and say so.

## Limits

- The check cannot tell that a figure is the one the source gives.
- A line with several figures needs one trace for all.
