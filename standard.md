# The review standard

What writing a literature review as nine files adds to the standard. It adds the files in which a reader and a program find a result, and the record that lets a reader redo the search, the scores and the figures. It extends [reader-ergonomics](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/0206e6950b4feca4194af419384c217863b7c42e/standard.md): read that one first. Its principles come from the [review of the genre](evidence/review-genre/README.md); most are conventions, and each says so.

## How to use it

1. **Frame, review, state of the art, position, baseline**, in that order, each to its [shape](shapes/README.md); a review is finished when it names its baseline.
2. **Write to the principles** below, and to those of reader-ergonomics, which still hold.
3. **Check:** run the standard's checks on the repository, and this repository's [audit](checks/README.md) on any other. `review-files` checks the nine files, `grey-subtotals` the grey scores, `review-numbers` the figures, `search-record` the strings and `second-rater` the agreement.

Grades mean what they mean in [reader-ergonomics](https://github.com/Tewf/reader-ergonomics/blob/0206e6950b4feca4194af419384c217863b7c42e/standard.md#grades): measured, supported with caveat, convention, not supported. Each principle states its grade and never claims more.

## Principles

<!-- principles:start -->

### Structure: what a review keeps

| Principle | Claim | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| [Five steps, in order](principles/structure/five-steps-in-order.md) | a review runs frame, review, state of the art, position and baseline in that order, and is finished when it can name the baseline it compares against. | convention, untested. No study compared a review in this order with one in another |
| [The nine files](principles/structure/the-nine-files.md) | a review keeps frame, protocol, sources, review, against, state of the art, position, baseline and references, one role each, so that a reader and a program find a result where it is kept. | convention, untested. The contents are asked for by guidelines; the split into files is the family's |

### Record: what a review lets a reader redo

| Principle | Claim | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| [Count every search](principles/record/count-every-search.md) | the protocol records every search string with its count, and says what was not searched, so that a reader can run the search again and compare. | convention, by consensus: PRISMA 2020 item 7 and the 16 items of PRISMA-S ask for it. No study measured that a recorded search is reproduced more often |
| [Log concepts per result](principles/record/log-concepts-per-result.md) | the protocol records, for each result screened and in order, whether it added a concept, so that a stopping estimator or a rule can judge when the search may stop. | supported with caveat for the estimator, as the data-gathering review graded it. A coverage estimate from singletons and doubletons (Chao, read in the linked review) is established in ecology. It was never applied to a literature search. Measured, descriptive, for the finding that none of the family's logs could feed it |
| [Report the second rater](principles/record/report-the-second-rater.md) | when two raters score or screen the same items, the review reports how far they agree, and how a disagreement was settled. | supported with caveat for a second reader on screening in health: single reviewers missed 8% of eligible reports on average, pairs 0 to 1% (Edwards 2002). Convention for a second rating of a quality score: no study was found |
| [Score grey sources in eight subtotals](principles/record/score-grey-in-eight-subtotals.md) | a grey source carries Garousi's eight subtotals and a total that adds up; below 10 of 20 it is an example of practice only, and says so. | convention. The 20 criteria are Garousi 2019's; the threshold of 10 is an example in that paper; no study measured how reliably two persons score a source |
| [Trace every figure](principles/record/trace-every-figure.md) | a figure in the review names its source and where it was read, or says it is secondary, so that a reader can find it. | convention. No study measured that located figures are checked more often |
