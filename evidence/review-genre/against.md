# Against

The evidence against what the front page assumes, and the disagreements. Sources are in [review](review.md). None is deleted to make the genre look stronger.

## Against "a review is nine files"

- PRISMA 2020 asks for one report with 27 items. Items 25 to 27 (support, competing interests, availability of data) have no file in the family's reviews (Page 2021, Table 1).
- Items 14, 15, 21 and 22 ask for bias from missing results and the certainty of the evidence. No file of the nine is named for them.
- The files against, position and baseline have no item in PRISMA or in Garousi 2019. They are the family's convention, with no evidence of their effect.

## Against "score the grey sources"

- A standard-setting body asks for grey sources without scoring them (Lefebvre 2025).
- The threshold of 10 of 20 is an example in the guideline, not a rule (Garousi 2019, section 5.2).
- Another group proposes a different quality checklist for grey sources (Yasin 2020, abstract, secondary).
- No study measures how reliably two persons score a source. In the family's first run, a second rater agreed on the threshold decision for 3 of 9 grey sources, kappa 0.07 ([human-ai-teaming protocol, section 12](https://github.com/Tewf/ai-augmented-project/blob/7b65bd3961f04fbc54e6bfa60cc5eabfccc77b38/evidence/human-ai-teaming/protocol.md)). One run on nine sources is an anecdote.

## Against "two readers, always"

- Single screening by an experienced reviewer is called a possible shortcut for rapid reviews (Waffenschmidt 2019, abstract, secondary).
- Cochrane's standard allows one person on titles and abstracts, and does not want searches duplicated (Lefebvre 2025).
- Percent agreement ignores chance (McHugh 2012, abstract, secondary). Chance agreement and unbalanced data skewed a model's scores as a screener (Khraisha 2024, abstract, secondary).

## Against "more search strings"

- Only 30% of one review's sources came from the protocol's searches (Greenhalgh 2005, abstract, secondary). A record of strings records the minority.
- Snowballing failed where the start set was poor (Badampudi 2015, abstract, secondary).

## Disagreements

| Point | One side | Other side |
|---|---|---|
| Snowballing against database search | 83% against 46% (Badampudi 2015) | comparable papers, in another comparison (Wohlin 2016) |
| Double screening | pairs missed none (Edwards 2002) | single by an experienced reviewer is acceptable (Waffenschmidt 2019) |
| Where the threshold comes from | 10 of 20 as a rule (the family) | 10 of 20 as an example (Garousi 2019) |

## Against the sources themselves

- Eighteen of 23 formal works were read from the abstract only.
- The screening evidence is from health. The snowballing evidence is mostly from one author's group in software engineering.
- Three sources were located and not read; two were not retrievable.
- The pool was capped at 25.
